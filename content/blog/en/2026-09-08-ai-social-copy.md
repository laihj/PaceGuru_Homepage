## The ten minutes every runner knows

You finish a 10K with great numbers: seven seconds per kilometer faster than last time, with a lower heart rate. You want to post it — and then—

You stare at the digits, not knowing where to start.

Write the pace? Friends who don't run won't know what "5:03" means. Write how it felt? "Felt good today" says nothing. Paste all the raw numbers? That reads like a work report.

So many runs worth remembering end up as a lone screenshot captioned "done."

## We wired copywriting into the sharing flow

The share menu on every run record now has a "Social Copy" entry. Tap it, and AI reads the run's key numbers — distance, pace, heart rate, plus how they compare to your recent form — then writes a caption you can post as-is.

Four styles to choose from:

- **Recommend**: evangelist energy, warm but not overhyped — for selling "running itself" to your friends
- **Minimal**: restrained data statements, one highlight per line, for daily-log style posts
- **Data-driven**: makes the progress legible — pace, heart rate, trend comparisons, the stuff runners get
- **Humor**: self-deprecation and light jokes, for "survived today's intervals" moments

## A few design decisions

### Title separate from body

Generated copy has its own headline, with the body below. Card-style previews on most platforms are built for this structure — the headline grabs attention, the body carries the detail. Mash it into one paragraph and everything disappears when the card collapses.

### Platform-neutral

We didn't tune for any specific platform — no built-in tone or hashtag stuffing for any particular app. The copy reads fine wherever you paste it. Where you post is your business; ours is telling the story of the run well.

### Follows your app's language

Whatever language your app is set to, that's the language the AI writes in. Chinese users get Chinese copy, Japanese users get Japanese — no "English app generates a Chinese post" mismatches.

### No repeated requests for the same run

Generated copy is cached. Open the same record again and you see the previous result instantly — no repeat request, no repeat wait. What you want is a fast copy, not watching the AI write it again.

## When to reach for it

In my own use, two scenarios stand out:

One is **a run with a story**. A new PB, a first time at some distance, holding it together through awful weather — the AI digs the progress out of the numbers and turns it into a proper record.

The other is **can't be bothered**. Everyday training doesn't have that much to say, but a continuous record is its own kind of meaning. Tap, pick a style, copy, post — thirty seconds for a record, which beats "never mind, not posting."
